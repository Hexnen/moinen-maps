# Moinen Maps

Foundry VTT v14 module: map notes become hand-inked animated pins (bouncy drop, hop, idle motion), standalone pins without notes from one toolbar tool with a pin palette (Classic, Fantasy and Modern presets, plus your own custom presets), and freehand painted "districts" with inked outlines, hatch/halftone fills, labels, attributes and hover effects. Configured per scene in the Pins tab of Scene Config.

The GM works from one scene-controls button, **Moinen Maps Tools**. Its second column holds the sections (Pins, Districts, Water, Roads, Tokens, and Destruction and Zones still to come); a section opens a workspace: its tools, the active tool's options and its actions in a bar across the top of the canvas, its lists and settings as collapsible panels in a dock left of the sidebar. Players get only the read-only list of revealed districts, when the scene allows it.

## Water

Paint rivers, lakes, ponds and waterfalls on the map and they become a seamlessly looping, animated pixel-art scene played between the map and the districts. Moinen Maps Tools → Water: three brushes (still water, river, fall), an eraser and the rod tool in the bar at the top, the water settings in the dock on the right.

- **Painting:** the brushes edit three masks; `[` and `]` change the brush size, `Ctrl+Z` undoes a stroke. A river that overlaps the top of a fall becomes the fall's mouth; a lake under a fall gets rings, foam and mist.
- **Water settings (dock panel):** every setting of the engine on six tabs. Look (16 looks, the six colours, six colours from one, clean pixel art), Water (shore foam, waves, shade, sparkle, net, patches, depth, drift, tide), River (speed, rush, rapids, streaks, way, net, banks, depth, patches, the mouth), Fall (style, speed, lines, weave, slits, top, arcs, edges, shading, light, splash, rings, reflection, trail, mist), Rods and Output (art pixel size, frames, frame time, opacity of every part). Changing colours or opacity never bakes again.
- **Rods:** bendable lines drawn on the map. Flow rods steer the water (with branches); lip rods draw the edge a fall goes over. Drag for a new rod, drag a dot to bend, drag the line for a new dot, Shift-drag an end dot to grow it, Alt-drag the line for a branch, right-click to remove.
- **Tokens in the water:** a token that stands in water gets pixel-art rings, one that enters or leaves it makes a splash, and one that moves leaves a foam wake carried by the current (the river's way, a flow rod or the drift). It is a light layer over the baked water, so nothing is baked again; switch it, its strength and who sees it in the Water tab.
- **Reflections of tokens:** an optional mirror image (or a black shadow) under every token, shown only where there is water, on the water's pixel grid. The same effects as Reflecto: offset, opacity, eleven blur shapes, ripple (amplitude, frequency, speed; loops with the water) and scanline shift; plus length, fade with distance and a tint from the water. Off by default; meant for side and oblique views.
- **Tokens under the water:** a token with an elevation below zero that stands in water is veiled by the water (darker the deeper it is, wavering) with bubbles rising from it. Nothing is baked and the token itself is untouched.
- **How it is shared:** the masks and settings are saved in the scene. A few seconds after the GM's last change the GM's client uploads the baked water as one PNG to `worlds/<world>/moinen-maps/` and players download it, with no baking, no worker and no waiting (about 66 KB for 16 frames of a small scene). A client that cannot get it (the GM has no upload right, or edited since) bakes the same seeded loop itself, and keeps the result in the browser for next time. The art pixel size (Output tab) sets how many scene pixels one pixel of the water art covers; smaller is finer and slower to bake.
- The waterfalls and the mouth are drawn for a side or oblique view (falling toward the bottom of the screen); lakes, ponds and rivers work from any angle (set the river's Way and the drift's direction).

## Tokens

How tokens enter and leave the scene, how they idle and how they die. Moinen Maps Tools → Tokens: a pick tool (click a token, Shift+click adds; Foundry's own selection is where it starts) and buttons that play an effect on the picked tokens on your screen only, plus one that marks them dead or alive (the core Dead status). The bar holds the effect in hand, its style for the scene, intensity, speed and "Set on picked"; the dock holds Entering, Leaving, Idle, Death, Targeting and combat (the older drop, hide / reveal, reticle and combat entry options, also still in the Scene Config Tokens fieldset) and the scene's tokens with their own styles.

- **Entering** (placed on the map or dragged in; revealed): drop, soft pop, fade in with dust, pop with a squash, materialise (its pixels fly in), warp in (a beam and a flash), slide in from the nearest scene edge, rise out of the ground, portal, none.
- **Leaving** (deleted; hidden): played on a copy of the token, so the deletion itself is never delayed: puff, dissolve into its pixels, fade with dust, shrink to a sparkle, warp out, sink, burst into confetti of its own colours, pixel smoke, slide off, none. A dead token does not leave again.
- **Idle:** breathe, bob, sway, wobble, hover (its shadow pulses), flicker, glow, wiggle on hover; strength, tempo, when (always, standing still, out of combat), stronger on its combat turn. The mesh transform only, a different phase per token, nothing off screen, paused during other effects and while dragged; off in Performance mode Low and with reduced motion.
- **Death:** a token is dead when it is marked defeated in the combat tracker, has the Dead status, or (off by default) its hit points reach 0 (the known path of the game system or one you type; in Invincible health 0 means broken). Fall over, collapse, grey and skull, disintegrate, burst (no gore), soul wisp, petrify, slump, melt; then the corpse look (lies as it fell, standing grey, faded out, unchanged) applied on every later draw, so a reload never plays the death again; revived tokens stand up again (or at once).
- **Own styles:** any token can override each style (flags.moinen-maps.fx); the token's own style beats the scene's, which beats the built-in. Nothing here writes a document while it plays: every client draws the same from the same hooks. Each player can switch token effects off for their own screen (module setting "Token effects on this client"). Bursts of more than 25 tokens at once are not animated.

## Roads

Paint roads on the map and little cars start driving on them. Moinen Maps Tools → Roads: the road pen and the eraser in the bar at the top (with the brush: road kind, width, lanes, one-way), the traffic and the road look in the dock.

- **Painting:** drag to paint a road freehand (it is smoothed into a curve), or click point by point and finish with a double-click, Enter, a click on the last point or a right-click. An end that stops near a road or junction joins it; where a road crosses another both get a junction. Alt-click a road to give it the current brush, Alt+Shift-click to turn a one-way road around, right-click to delete a road. The eraser cuts roads where you drag it. `[` and `]` change the width (or the eraser size), `Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Shift+Z` undo and redo.
- **Roads already drawn on the map:** trace them with the **Guide line** kind (`G` switches the pen between a guide line and the last painted kind). A guide line is never painted: the map's own road shows and the cars drive on it. Set its width to the road on the map (the cars keep to its lanes). Only the GM sees guide lines (a faint corridor with a dashed centre), while a road tool is in hand or always (Road look → Guide lines); players see only the cars. Guide lines join painted roads and each other like any road. Freehand traces are smoothed harder; hold Shift while clicking points for straight spans at 15° steps.
- **Editing shapes (Edit tool):** click a road to select it; its control points show as handles (the road is a smooth curve through them). Drag a point to bend the road; drag an end and the roads joined there follow, and an end dropped on another road or junction joins it. Drag the line to pull out a new point (a click adds one), right-click a point or press Delete to remove it, double-click a point to make it a sharp corner (and back). Shift-drag the line to move the whole road, Shift-drag a dead end to extend it. The options bar shows the selected road's kind, width, lanes, one-way, its own speed, turn it around or delete it; `[` `]` change its width. The roads bake live while you drag; every change is one undo step.
- **Sizes:** roads from 0.05 to 3 squares wide (markings and crossings are left out where a road is too narrow to read them; a road narrower than two cars side by side, or one on a one-way road, carries no traffic) and cars from 0.03 square long, with an optional random size variation.
- **Kinds:** asphalt (kerbs, dashed centre line, one-way chevrons, zebra crossings at junctions), cobblestones (stone by stone), dirt road (ruts, pebbles, a frayed rim) and a footpath (no cars). Inked like the districts, with a soft ground shadow; drawn above the water (a road over a river is a bridge), under the districts' tint, tiles and tokens.
- **Traffic:** seven kinds of tiny top-down cars (small car, sedan, van, pickup, lorry, bus, taxi) in nine colours, inked or as pixel art. They keep to the right (or left), slow down for curves, keep their distance, take turns at junctions (the one that waited longest goes first, and nobody drives into a full junction), turn round at dead ends and drive off the map where a road leaves it, coming back in elsewhere. Headlights and tail lights at night (automatic with the scene's darkness, or always on/off), brake lights, a little sway on rough roads. Density, speed, car size, a cap on the number of cars, which vehicles, and who sees them (everyone or only the GM) are set per scene.
- **How it is shared:** the roads and settings are one small scene flag. Nothing else is ever sent: every client runs the same seeded simulation from the server clock, so the GM and the players see the same cars in the same places. The simulation restarts from a new seeded layout every 20 minutes (the cars cross-fade); a client that joins steps from the start of that period, which takes a few frames.
- Reduced motion (the module's setting with the system preference) keeps the cars moving but drops their sway and flicker.

## Zones

Zones for Year Zero style movement (counted in zones, not squares). In the **Zones** section the GM draws loose pencil lines: any
area the lines close off becomes a zone. Lines may overshoot and cross; an end that stops just short of another line is joined to it
("Join gaps"). **Trim** cuts away every bit of line that does not bound a zone; the eraser rubs lines out; Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z.
Inside the section the GM sees the raw lines; everywhere else (and for players) only the finished zones show: a soft tint per zone,
dotted borders and zone numbers (who sees them: everyone / GM / nobody).

The distance between two zones is the number of borders on the shortest route. With one token selected every zone shows its
distance and what reaching it costs under the scene's movement rules, and the token ruler shows zones instead of squares.
First preset: **Invincible** (quick action = 1 zone on foot, two quick actions = 2 zones; movement powers 2/4, Major 4/8,
Massive 8/16, Monstrous 16/32, read from the actor's power items). API: `game.modules.get("moinen-maps").api.zones`.

Look: tint, border width and opacity, colour, zone numbers (always / on hover / never, centred or in the top-left corner, fixed on
the map while zooming) and an optional highlight of the zone under the cursor (bright border plus an inner glow, or hatching,
crosshatch or dots that sit along the border and gather in the corners; its own opacity). **Presets**: look presets (Classic,
Subtle, Sketch, Bold, plus any the GM saves with *Save as preset*) and movement presets. *World defaults* sets the look and movement
preset for every map; a map follows them (*World default*) or picks its own preset, and changing any look setting on a map makes it
that map's own look. Who sees the zones is always the map's own choice.

## Destruction

Paint damage on the map; every brush stroke is an object of its own with a damage kind and a level (cracked, broken, crater).

- **Neighbours** (the footprint rule): a stroke owns its own pixels; there a newer stroke replaces what older damage lay under it. Around its own pixels it only touches the ground (its halo, scorch, the cause's stain, moss or rime, the inked lip, hairline cracks, the shadows of its pieces, the pieces it throws): those darken, stain or ink what lies there, older damage included, and never lay clean ground over it. Declared reach beyond the mask: blast about 0.9 of its radius, scorch 1.4 of it plus 4 art px, collapse half of it, a cause's halo its own share, hairlines up to about its radius; thrown pieces land on top. A tear's lips stay inside its own stroke. An older stroke never changes because of a newer one (its bytes and look stay). Checked for every pair of kinds by `dev/destruction-footprint.test.mjs`.
  - **What it is painted on** (the substrate, `scripts/destruction/substrate.mjs`): inside its own pixels a newer stroke is made out of the older damage under it, not out of the clean map. A tear across a cave-in tears the pit (its lips are the pit's slabs, its floor goes deeper than the pit), across a crater it shows the crater's rim, across burnt ground the lips are charred, across cracks the cracks are pulled apart with it, across a flooded hole it only darkens the water (water does not tear); a blast over cracks throws the cracked plates; a scorch chars what lies there; a flooded hole over a pit or a crater fills deeper there. Where a stroke lays nothing (a tear beside its opening, plates a blast left in place) the older damage stays as it is, live: a stroke never lays clean ground inside its own pixels. The table `INTERACT` says per (newer, older) kind how the floor treats what is under it (deepen, char, leave it to the older look, deeper water).
  - **One way, no reloads**: a newer stroke depends only on the older strokes it reads - those whose torn outline can reach its own pixels, a higher level within its reach, erase strokes - never the other way, and it reads their plain looks (made from their own data alone), so no stroke waits for another's bake (a cold bake is two waves on the worker pool). Its signature is its own data plus a digest of those (ids and their own signatures), so painting a stroke bakes only it; changing an older stroke (its cause, its pixels) or removing it bakes again only the strokes over it, and they are swapped in within one frame (no entry, no fade, no blank frame); erasing and undo bake nothing. Every client composes the same substrate from the same bakes (the older strokes' chunks), so the published chunks hold; a stroke whose chunk does not match is baked here alone, over the records of the strokes under it.
  - **Severity** (the level is a real gradation): where strokes overlap, the higher level wins at every pixel (the painted level of each stroke there). A lower level never covers, whitens or dilutes a higher one: hairline cracks painted over a crater stop at the crater, their halo, lip, hairlines and thrown pieces are clipped off its pixels (cracks wholly inside a crater bake nothing). A higher level over a lower one replaces it, as before. So the overlap of two levels looks the same in either paint order (only the entries differ); at the same level the newer one lies on top and is made of the older one. Each kind grows darker, deeper and wider from level 1 to 3, and a crater is deeper than a level-2 cave-in. Checked by `dev/destruction-substrate.test.mjs` (one way, digests, bake counts, no clean ground under a tear, every level pair, paint order, gradation per kind and cause).

- **Cause** (*Przyczyna*): what did the damage, a third choice next to the kind and the level, and independent of both: **fire**, **acid**, **lightning**, **arcane**, **impact** (meteor, giant fist), **claws** (beasts, blades), **water** (erosion), **time and rot** and **frost**. A kind says what form the damage takes, the cause how it got there: cracks + fire are glowing charred cracks, a collapse + time and rot crumbles slowly under moss, a blast + acid is an acid splash crater. The cause recolours what lies beneath and the pieces from the map's own colours, sets how the ground fractures (more spokes, spirals, strips along the stroke, 60° ice crystals, wandering roots, branching bolts, parallel scratches running past the damage), the lip, the halo, the exposed floor and small specks - each cause its own silhouette, not a tint: claws cut 3-4 tapering parallel furrows with turf ridges between them, an impact leaves a compact pale crater with radial fractures and a lit pressed crest, water stands in an eroded pit (a calm blue-green surface, a meniscus, ripples, sediment along the flow), frost a faceted ice sheet (30/90/150° facets) with a few shards, acid round bubbling pits and a fading green-yellow bleed, rot a sagging slump with moss on its lit slopes, lightning a fused-glass core and one fused line along the bolt, fire char with glowing fissures, arcane a broken ring of sigils. A tear takes the cause's shape too: claws three parallel rips, impact a star of radial rips, frost a straight rift with 60° crystal spurs, water a meandering channel, acid a ragged eaten slit, rot a sagging frayed split, arcane an angular rift, fire a wide charred split, lightning a zig-zag slit. Fire, acid, lightning and arcane damage glows: in a dark scene its embers, bolts and runes keep their colour and light the ground a little, like the districts' night lights. In a dark scene (from darkness 0.5, full at 0.85) EVERY damage also gets a rim light in the scene's illumination - a thin light edge on its lips and the lit bevels beside its cracks, in the cause's colour (moonlit grey, dust, water blue, moss green, ice, ember, violet...) - so a black crack on dark ground still reads; nothing changes by day. The cause also has its own entry: fire heat shimmer, a rising ember swirl and a scorched ring creeping out (no starburst); impact a shadow tightening onto the spot, then a broken white shock ring, radial fractures, a 2-px thump and chips; claws its streaks cutting in one after another; water ripples welling up; acid bubbles swelling and popping; rot flakes falling in and spores; frost crystal needles at 30/90/150°; lightning a branching bolt and a few flicker arcs on its trail; arcane a turning ring of sigils; rot creeps in slowly. And the idle life. Pick it in the bar (Shift+0–9, `,` and `.` step through); **Scene default** stores no cause on the stroke, which then follows the **Default cause** of the Look panel (none: the kind's plain look, exactly as before causes existed). The Damage panel lists every stroke with its cause; changing it there bakes only that stroke again. Macro: `api.destruction.paint(x, y, r, { type, level, cause: "fire" })`, `api.destruction.setCause(id, "frost")`.

- **Tears** (*Rozdarcia*): the surface ripped open along the stroke, like torn paper, cloth, turf or a split deck. The brush path is the tear's spine (without one, the painted shape's axis): an elongated opening, widest a little off centre and pointed at both tips. Cracked is a hairline rip, broken an open tear, crater a gaping rip into darkness. The two lips never match: the map beside them is pulled apart and sheared (whatever crossed the tear no longer lines up), each lip has its own torn edge and shows a thin rim of its underside, and the lip's texture follows the material: turf tears in lumps with soil under it, stone chips in steps, wood splinters, metal peels with a curled edge, paper, cloth and earth fray. Below: the far wall in strata, the north lip's shadow, a floor that darkens with depth, roots, joists or a girder. Fibres and strands hang across the gap (some whole and sagging, most snapped), flaps of the surface hang into it, short secondary rips branch off obliquely near the tips and a hairline runs on past each tip; chips lie along the lips. Torn borders make the lips rougher. The entry: a pinprick, then a fast tearing front along the spine (both ways from the puncture, or from the stroke's start at the drawing speed with "follows the stroke"), the lips flutter, peel apart with a small curl and snap into place, fibres snap, crumbs of the material spill and each tip snaps; reduced motion fades it in.
- **Flooded** (*Zalane*): the hole fills with the module's own pixel-art water, the same engine and look as painted water, animated in a loop on the same clock as the scene's water. The level decides how much water: **cracked** is damp ground with water seeping up in the cracks and a few puddles (the water rises and sinks back in the cracks); **broken** is a shallow pool, see-through so the bottom, the rubble and the sunk plates show, a little deeper inside; **crater** is a deep pool, clear at its broken rim and opaque and darker toward the middle. The engine draws the shore foam where the water meets the rim, the waves, sparkles and its own deep zone. The water comes in after the break: it wells up from the deepest points (and from the cracks), the level rises to the rim with a line of foam on it and rings spread from the springs; with reduced motion it fades in. Tokens treat a flooded hole as water: wading rings, a splash on the way in and out, the foam wake, the veil and bubbles under the water (elevation below zero) and, when the scene's water has reflections on, reflections.
  - **Colours:** the scene's painted water's look, colours and overall opacity (`alpha.all` and every part's opacity); changing them repaints the holes, a new look bakes them again. A pool (broken, crater) beside painted water also takes the painted water's pixel size, on the same grid, so the two read as one body of water at the border; cracked strokes keep a finer grid for their thin cracks. A scene without painted water gets colours made from the map (a muddy blue from its average colour, by the engine's palette maker; the GM stores them in the scene) and a water pixel of 16 per grid square.
  - **How it is baked and shared:** every flooded area (one stroke's connected part) has its own small loop, baked in a water-engine worker of its own from that area alone (its torn outline, its open pixels, its levels), so painting, erasing or undoing one stroke bakes only that stroke's water, never another hole and never the painted water (about 50 ms for a hole two squares wide). The loops are kept in the browser and the GM's client publishes each one (level bytes, like the painted water); players download them and bake nothing. The painted water and the holes never share a bake. API: `api.destruction.flood` (`status()`, `at(x, y)`, `replay()`, `rebake()`).

- **Bake versions: how updates affect existing damage** (0.4.2, version pinning, `scripts/destruction/bake-policy.mjs`): a published stroke keeps the look it was baked with, whatever later updates change in the code - an update of the module never re-bakes a scene by itself. A stroke is baked again only when it changes (its pixels, kind, cause, path, the settings, the strokes it is painted over), when its chunk is missing or unreadable, or when you ask. A new stroke is baked with the current look. The Damage panel says how many strokes keep the look of an older version (counted per kind: a tear-only change makes only tears "older") and offers **Rebake all** (in the background, nearest to the view first, a couple of strokes at a time, each new look swapped in silently; pause, resume, cancel) and a per-stroke rebake button in the list. Scenes published by 0.4.1 and before are taken over as they are (their entries keep the version they were baked with). For module developers: every bump of `CORE_VERSION` adds a line `NN: { kinds: [...], note: "..." }` to `BAKE_VERSIONS`, naming what it changed - kinds `blast`, `collapse`, `scorch`, `flood`, `cracks`, `gash`, `cause` (the cause treatments) or `global`; `dev/bake-policy.test.mjs` refuses an entry without `kinds`.

- **Carry, hovering tokens and moinen-pixel-vfx** (Ctrl+drag carries a token along a stroke; `tokens/carry.mjs`): with moinen-pixel-vfx >= 0.2.0 the carry uses its official *visual transform* API (`game.modules.get("moinen-pixel-vfx").api.visual`, contract in that module's `dev/visual-transform.md`): every frame it declares the ride's offset and the pose lift (source `moinen-maps-carry`, `owned: true`: the carry poses the mesh itself) and clears it at the hand-over, so the passives, auras, the flight cape, adaptive bodies, afterimage trails and ground shadows follow, the ground shadow stays on the floor, and no probe is needed. Without the API (an older moinen-pixel-vfx) `tokens/carry-fx.mjs` keeps the old probe heuristic as the fallback; without the module nothing happens. **Height:** a hovering / flying token (its flight or levitation passive, or `api.visual.baseHeight`) keeps its height all the way: the base is taken from Foundry's natural mesh place and the live hover is added back every frame under the pose lift (`carry-core.liftedY`), so there is no step at the grab, the landing or the hand-over. Without `api.visual` (moinen-pixel-vfx 0.1.x, the motion table writes a held mesh every frame at the document's centre plus its hover) the carry READS that displacement late in each frame (a mesh that is not where the carry left it was rewritten; an early 0.25 px nudge makes that visible even when both write the same value) and rides it on top of the pose (`carry-core.extOf` / `rewritten`), also in the frame the carry ends. The touch-down continues the flight's last pose (lean, stretch, lift) and fades into the landing over 200 ms (`carry-core.blendLanding`): the lean no longer snaps upright at the path's end. **Superhero** only meets tokens in its own height band (document elevation + the vfx hover + a flying status, `plough-core.heightCells`, band 0.5 cell): a ground carrier passes under a hovering token and a flying carrier over a ground one. Setting *Superhero ignores height* (off by default) ploughs them all; a pushed hovering token then keeps its own height (it does not snap to the carrier's). Heights are read once, when the stroke is committed. Z-order is Foundry's (elevation first, then the carry's rank); a hover that exists only as a vfx effect (no document elevation) does not change the draw order.

### Fire and smoke

A layer of its own over the destruction layer (`scripts/fire/`): paint burning fire and drifting smoke on the map, independent of the damage strokes.

- **Tools** (Destruction section): **Fire brush**, **Smoke brush**, **Eraser**. `[` `]` change the brush size, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z undo and redo whole strokes. The bar has strength, size, wind speed and the wind direction (8 arrows), and level buttons in destruction's style: the fire brush shows its **fire level** (1 smoulder, 2 fire, 3 blaze; keys 1 2 3) and, beside it, its own **smoke level** (off, 1 wisps, 2 plume, 3 column; Shift+0..3) plus its **look** (fire / fireball) and its **colour** (Scene, Natural, Hot, Red, Blue gas, Alchemical, Arcane, White-hot, or a custom colour); the smoke brush shows its smoke level (keys 1 2 3). Each control remembers its value (client settings). The smoke brush paints smoke alone. **Torn edges** (the wave button in the bar, or *Torn edges* + *Tearing* in the Fire and smoke panel; as destruction's torn borders): new fire and smoke strokes get a ragged footprint - lobes, inlets, chipped bites, varying width and a few detached islands near the edge - instead of a round brush; even without it every new stroke has an organic edge. Each stroke keeps what it was painted with (strokes painted before keep their round brush); the stroke list's wave button turns it on or off for one stroke. Macro: `api.fire.paint(pts, { torn: true })`, `api.fire.setTorn(id, 0.8)`. Fields are computed in a worker (`scripts/fire/raster-worker.mjs`): painting, releasing, undo / redo and strokes from other clients never stall a frame; the previous field stays on screen until the new one is ready.
- **Levels and strength**: the level is the kind of fire or smoke (each level carries its own coverage, density, opacity, height and edge ramps in the look block; level 1 is delicate and shows most of the ground, level 3 covers the most). Strength is the fine tuning within a level (how much of it). The lights follow the fire level only (radius and intensity). Strokes from before the levels read as fire 2, smoke 2.
- **Looks**: *fire* (default) burns in place, drawn as pixel art on the art grid at 12 fps (`scripts/fire/art.mjs`): blob flames from emitters over a glowing ember bed, gathered by a slowly wandering fuel map into uneven clusters (big merged masses, mid clumps, single tongues and gaps with only embers, each base its own width and glow; they swell, die back and creep over seconds), merged into one flame body (tongues fuse and fork, colour bands flow across it, outline only on its outer silhouette), glowing coals between the tongues and past the bed's edge (they outlast the flames), sparks rising with the heat and cooling through the palette (more with the level and in gusts), breathing and travelling gusts, a ground multiply (char, heat tint, smoke shadow), smoke as lit 3-lobe puff billows that start above the flames (a thin warm veil where they still overlap); flames are emissive in the dark (they write into Foundry's illumination), smoke is not. *fireball* is the layer's first look, kept unchanged (`dev/fire-fireball-identity.test.mjs` checks its field against a frozen copy in `dev/fixtures/fire-v1`) in `scripts/fire/shader.mjs`. Per stroke; different looks never share an area. A look can be swapped without touching the mechanics (model fields, times, levels, colours, lights).
- **Colour**: each fire stroke stores its base colour (`c`; without one it takes the scene's *Fire colour*, natural orange by default, which old strokes keep). It is not a tint: `scripts/fire/look.mjs` (`rampOf`, the swappable rule) derives the whole palette from the base - core, hot, mid, outer, tips, bed glow, embers, char, the smoke's lit underside, the ember particles and the light - with hue shifts and value steps, so a blue fire still has a pale cyan core and dark indigo tips. The natural colour gives the old palette exactly (both looks, fireball included). Each light takes its fire's colour (the scene's light colour is an override when *Light in the fire's colour* is off). The smoke stays neutral unless the scene's *Smoke tint* mixes it towards its fire's hue. Fires of different colours never share an area or a light.
- **Growing and going out**: a new stroke catches along its path in drawing order, the flames grow, the smoke builds after them, the light grows. Erase / delete / Clear / `remove`: the flames sink into the bed, embers linger and darken, the smoke lingers and drifts on, the light fades. Undo and redo play the same. All of it follows from the shared clock and the stroke's paint (`q`), relight (`g`) and out (`o`) times: no per-frame writes, a joiner sees the right phase, and when an animation ends the GM removes the put-out records in one write (nothing replays, nothing extra at rest). Reduced motion: an instant change. Durations per scene (dock): *Catching*, *Growing*, *Going out*.
- **Areas**: strokes merge into one area by their burning core only (a plume reaching another fire does not merge them, so distinct fires keep distinct lights); the area is sized to hold the whole plume, which fades to nothing inside it in every wind (`dev/fire-model.test.mjs` I).
- **Data**: every stroke is an object of its own in the scene flag `fireStrokes` (kind fire / smoke / erase, strength, radius, path, wind); the layer's settings in the flag `fire`. Nothing is baked or uploaded: every client computes the same areas and the same frame from the strokes and the shared clock.
- **Look**: one GPU quad per fire area, drawn on the destruction layer's art grid (the same art pixel and origin), procedural noise quantised to whole art cells and a small palette (flame tongues rising, a dark plume carried along the wind that thins out by ordered dither, glowing specks on the burning ground). Crisp at any zoom and pixel density with the module's sharp sampler rule (`CONFIG.moinenMaps.zoomAA`). On top a few embers and smoke puffs, each a pure function of the clock (no state, no spawning), capped by the Performance mode (high 48, medium 24, low 0). Z-order: above the damage, the districts and the cars (sortLayer 256), below tiles and tokens.
- **Light**: the fire lights the scene through Foundry's own lighting - a few client-side light sources (never AmbientLight documents) made from the burning ground, nearby fire merged into one light (at most 24 per scene), blocked by walls, lighting tokens and visible to players' tokens across the dark. The flicker is the "torch" light shader driven by the shared clock: it never refreshes the lighting. By day (darkness below 0.2) the fire does not light. Settings per scene: light on/off, radius (a multiple of the fire area), intensity, colour, flicker.
- **Fire panel** (dock): on/off, who sees it (everyone / GM only), flame height, plume length, smoke, speed, catching / growing / going out, fire colour, smoke tint, the light settings (light in the fire's colour or the light colour), the list of strokes (its colour, its levels F#/S#, click to step them; delete one) and Clear.
- **Cost**: nothing painted = nothing made (no container, no ticker, no light). With fire: one ticker listener (~0.01-0.02 ms a frame) that culls areas off screen and writes one uniform per visible area at 12 frames a second. Reduced motion: a still frame, steady light, no particles, no ticker.
- **Macro**: `api.fire.paint([[x, y], ...], { kind: "fire", radius: 60, strength: 0.8, level: 2, smoke: 2, style: "fire", color: "blue", wind: { dir: 270, speed: 0.4 } })` (fire: `level` 1-3, `smoke` 0-3; smoke brush: `smoke` 1-3; `color` "#rrggbb" or a preset name, omitted = the scene's), `setLevels(id, { level, smoke })`, `setColor(id, color)` (null = the scene's), `colors()`, `rampOf(color)`, `erase(x, y, r)`, `remove(id)`, `clear()`, `undo()`, `redo()`, `list()`, `params()`, `setParams({ light: true, lightRadius: 1.5 })`, `status()`. Another source of fire (a destruction stroke that burns, later) passes a mask through `api.fire.model.fieldFromMask` and shows it with `api.fire.setExtra(id, area, field)`. Kill switch: `localStorage["mm-fire"] = "off"`.

## Weather

A layer of its own for the whole scene (`scripts/weather/`): rain, snow, a sandstorm, dust and embers in the module's oblique view, a ground that gets wet (and dries) or snowed on (and melts), puddles, and what each does on the camera's glass (the lens). Every client draws the same frame from the scene flag and the shared clock; nothing is simulated or sent per frame.

- **Weather section** (workspace, GM): the weather type (Clear, Rain, Snow, Sandstorm, Dust, Embers, Wind, Storm = rain + wind, Blizzard = snow + wind), the **level** 1-3 (keys 1 2 3: level 1 is delicate and shows most of the map, 3 covers the most) and the **intensity** within the level; the **wind** (8 direction buttons, strength; or the *Wind* tool: drag an arrow on the map, four squares = full strength); the **transition** time (how long a change takes; the old weather blends into the new one, a late joiner sees the right phase); the **lens** on/off and its level (*auto* follows the world level). The dock: on/off and who sees it (everyone / GM only), the wind (direction, strength, gusts), the ground (how fast it gets wet and dries, puddles on/off, how fast snow settles and melts, *Dry now*), the lens (on, level, strength, heat on/off), the view angle (top, oblique, steep; shared with fire), and this client's own caps. A scene that also has Foundry's core weather gets a warning (both would draw).
- **Rain**: instanced 1-px streaks in three depth layers on the art grid, each ending on its own ground point (lifted by the view angle), with splash crowns and droplets where it lands and rings on water and puddles. Density comes in uneven patches that drift with the wind and never loop; gusts travel across the map and slant the rain. Streaks stop under roofs (tiles with *Restrict weather*, by Foundry's depth mask) and in regions with the *Suppress Weather* behaviour; the ground there stays dry.
- **Snow**: flakes in three depth layers swaying down with a swirl and a small shadow that shows their height, carried by the wind; a cold tint; at level 3 spindrift runs along the ground in the gusts. Snow settles on the land (10 % at level 2, 42 % at level 3, a thin dusting first), in patches that favour flat, bright ground, using the map's own lines; it builds while it snows and melts after (rates in the dock), like the wet ground. The share is the scene's own: the threshold is a quantile of the shader's score over a sample of the map, so it holds on any map.
- **Sandstorm**: ochre grit hopping along the ground in the waves (fast, the strongest in the gusts), ground streamers that move with the wind, sand settling in drifts in the lee (5 / 12 / 18 % of the land), an ochre tint and, from level 2, a haze above the tokens (level 3: the wall, in lit and shadow bands).
- **Dust** (still air): slow motes that glint where diagonal sun shafts fall, a warm tint, and at level 3 a dusty haze.
- **Embers**: a wind-borne field of sparks blown in from upwind: they come in from the edge and from everywhere in view, drift through with the wind and its sway, cool through the ember colours and die by pixel dropout. They travel in swarms (a slow patchy field drifting with the wind, stronger in the gust fronts), never from fixed spots. They have depth: far sparks are dim, cooler and see-through and cross the screen slower; near ones are bright, opaque and fast, with a trail. Level 1 a few stray sparks, 2 drifting sparks with a light smoke haze, 3 an ember storm. A dim, warm evening tint. (Sparks rising from real fire areas belong to the fire layer.)
- **Wind** (visible wind, from the scene wind: its direction, strength and gusts, at least a breeze): wind lines - light 1-px strokes curving downwind in the gust fronts, growing and retracting; leaves and bits tumbling with the wind, flipping, bouncing more in the gusts, each with its ground shadow, coming in from upwind and from everywhere; grass combed by the gusts (bands of lighter blades running downwind over the green); water ruffled (darker cat's paws running with the gusts, broken light dashes in them); cloud shadows drifting over everything from level 2; blown dust drifting along the wind at level 3; a slight dim. Level 1 a breeze (rare lines in the strongest gusts, a few leaves, combing only in the strongest gusts), 2 a moderate wind, 3 a strong gusty wind. Nothing on the lens (air is invisible on glass). Storm and Blizzard add it to rain or snow at the same level.
- **Tornado** (its own weather object, up to 3 per scene; the *Tornado* tool in the Weather section: click a spot for one that stays and wanders there, or drag a path it travels along; the *Tornadoes* dock panel sets the next one's level, speed and what it does at the end of the path - turn back, stop, or rope out - and lists the scene's tornadoes, each with its level 1-3 and *Rope out*, plus *Rope out all*): a rotating storm deck lowers a funnel that touches down, travels and ropes out, all smoothly. The funnel is flexible: it bends with the path (it lags, whips when the path turns), snakes and breathes, with bands running up it; its top flares into the turning wall cloud of the deck. The base is a debris cloud and a dust skirt with radial jets and rolling rings. Debris is cut from the map under it: map fragments, planks and clumps are lifted, spun round and thrown out, landing with a scar and a shadow. Level 1 is a thin snaking rope, 2 a cone with a debris cloud, 3 a wide wedge with a rain curtain, lightning in the deck and the most debris. Its wind joins the scene wind: fire areas lean into it smoothly (the lean eases in over ~0.5 s, swings through as the core passes and recovers gradually; only the flames' lean changes, never the fire's own wind, so the fire does not jump), flames in front of its foot are drawn over the funnel and flames behind it stay under it, rain, snow and other weather particles near it swirl round it (`api.weather.tornado.windAt(x, y)`). Everything is a pure function of the stored path and the shared clock: every client sees the same tornado at the same place. Cost: nothing while there is none; one ticker, one art-resolution texture and ~70 / 260 / 470 instances (L1 / 2 / 3), ~0.4-0.6 ms CPU a frame on the dev machine, also with a long drawn path (the smoothed path is cached per stored path; it was rebuilt from a string key every call, up to ~4 ms a frame); the funnel volume skips rays that never meet it and its ground is cell-cached when zoomed in. Macro: `api.weather.tornado.add([[x, y], ...], { level, speed, end })`, `update(id, { level, end })`, `remove(id)`, `clear()`, `list()`, `positionAt(id, t)`. (`damage` is stored for a later optional map-damage hookup; it is off and does nothing yet.)
- **Rain and snow over fire**: fire under rain burns lower with fewer tongues, its smoke turns white-grey and its light gets weaker, by level (snow less); white-grey steam rises where rain meets the burning cells and mixes with the smoke. It follows the weather transitions smoothly and changes only the fire's uniforms and lights (no re-raster). Optional, per scene (Ground panel, off by default): *Rain puts fire out* - after 90 / 45 / 20 s of rain at level 1 / 2 / 3 the active GM puts the burning fire out (one write, undoable; snow never does). Roofs do not shelter fire from this yet. Cost: nothing without rain or fire; one instanced draw per fire area for the steam (~0.1 ms GPU on the dev machine). Macro: `setParams({ fire: { out: true } })`.
- **Wet ground**: the map's own colours darken and cool as it gets wet (no copy of the map), flat open ground gathers puddles with a lit far rim, and a few glints catch the light. The wetness builds while it rains and dries after (rates in the dock), the same on every client.
- **Lens (rain on the glass)**: beads of many sizes (mostly small, a few big), each born with a splat; runners that grow, break loose and slide down in jerks along a wobbly path slanted by the wind, leaving a wet streak that beads up and wipes the misty film; mist specks and a cool film at the edges. Drops are clear water: a big drop shows what lies under it - the map, tokens, fire and the weather - at full resolution, slightly magnified and pulled at the edge, with a faint darker crescent below and a glint of light along its upper-left rim (a full-resolution copy of the frame, one blit each frame while there are drops; without it the drop is clear and undistorted); a small drop is only a faint lift and a glint. The glint is a soft crescent of light that follows the drop's curve, white-hot at the core and cooler at its edge, larger on bigger drops. The wet trail behind a runner is a thin band of water whose width wavers, which pulls apart into pieces as it dries, with a thin broken line of light along its edge. Nothing on the glass is drawn in squares or blocks, by day or at night: drops and their edges, trails, mist specks, frost, crystals, sand grains and scratches, dust specks and ember flecks are all drawn smooth and antialiased per CSS pixel (sizes and densities are set in lens units of about 3 CSS px, fixed to the screen whatever the zoom or pixel density). The lens is drawn 12 times a second at one pixel per CSS pixel, spread over the frames between two steps in four bands (two below 48 fps, all at once below 24), and scaled smoothly to the screen each frame. Level 1 is a few beads and a rare runner, level 2 adds more runners, mist and film, level 3 is the full wet glass with runners sliding often.
- **Lens, other weathers** (only what happens on the glass): *snow*: frost growing in from the edges and corners in ice cells with feathered tips, crystals that land on the glass and melt into beads (not on the frost); *sandstorm*: a fine deposit on the wind side and lower edge that thickens in the waves, scratches that catch the light, grit impacts (a flash, then a chipped pit that stays a while); *dust*: veiling glare and flare ghosts from a low sun off the corner (breathing as thin clouds pass), out-of-focus dust discs and specks that glint, a smudge whose ridges light up in the glare; *embers* (*heat*, off by default): a heat glow from the fire side and hot flecks that hit the glass and cool to ash.
- **Per player** (client settings): *Weather detail* (off, 1, 2, 3: the highest level this client draws) and *Lens effects* (off, 1, 2, 3). Performance Low caps the world at level 2 and turns the lens off; reduced motion halves the density and turns the lens off.
- **Wind for other layers**: the weather's wind is the scene's wind: `api.weather.windAt(x, y)`.
- **Cost**: no weather = nothing made (no mesh, no ticker). With weather: one ticker listener (a few uniforms; the state is resolved at most 12 times a second), one draw call per pass that has something to draw (ground; rain splashes and air; one per particle kind; the haze) and 1 more for the lens; the lens pass runs 12 times a second at one pixel per CSS pixel, in four bands spread over the frames between its steps, after two tiny pre-passes that work out the step's runners (per column) and beads (per cell) once instead of in every pixel (rain level 3 on the dev machine's RTX 3070 Ti, a 1584 x 805 CSS px window: ~0.47 ms GPU per step, ~0.1 ms per frame; the pass at lens resolution before was ~0.64 ms per step; without float render targets the pass works them out itself). The ground and the haze are one value per map cell: zoomed in (a cell over 1.6 device px across: zoom 2+ at DPR 1, zoom 1+ at DPR 2) each is computed once per cell into a float texture and the screen pass only reads it, which cuts them to a fraction at zoom 3 and at DPR 2 with the same pixels (`CONFIG.moinenMaps.weatherCells = false` turns this off). **Responsiveness first**: panning and zooming were measured with the heaviest weather (rain + lens, sand, wind, a tornado on a path, storm; level 3; DPR 1 and 2; day and night; `dev/weather-move.mjs`): the weather usually adds under 1 ms of CPU and a few ms of GPU to a frame on the dev machine, with no read-backs during a move; frames over 16.7 ms (at DPR 2) and late frames came about as often with the weather off as on. Rare single spikes remain (a tornado debris step of 10-17 ms about once per few thousand frames; a cell texture remade when a zoom crosses the cell threshold). The display's per-frame step and the tornado's debris, path and axis code reuse their arrays (no per-instance garbage; the tornado's CPU per frame fell by about a quarter to a third). **Cold start**: when the browser's shader cache misses (a first visit, a cleared cache, a changed shader) linking one weather program blocks the page for 0.3-4 s, so a weather's first frame could freeze for seconds. The weather therefore pre-warms its programs after a scene loads, where the weather can show for this client: each is compiled and linked off the main thread (KHR_parallel_shader_compile) and then handed to PIXI, which hits the cache (a few ms, one per frame). `CONFIG.moinenMaps.weatherPrewarm = false` turns this off. A frame guard watches this client's frame times: when frames keep coming late (about one in five over the last ~16 frames; a lone hitch or a tab switch does not count), the weather thins its drops, particles and tornado debris and narrows its ground filter, gradually and down to about a third. It comes back slowly after 2 s of on-time frames. The guard is per client and never stored (`api.weather.guard()` shows it; `CONFIG.moinenMaps.weatherGuard = false` turns it off).
- **Data**: the scene flag `weather` (a short timeline of keys: preset, level, intensity, per-effect overrides, wind; plus the layer settings) and `view` (the view angle). Old keys fold into a base state, so the flag stays small.
- **Macro**: `api.weather.set("rain", { level: 3, over: 20000 })`, `level(2)`, `wind({ dir: 90, str: 0.5, gust: 0.6 })`, `lens({ on: true, level: "auto", strength: 1 })`, `clear()`, `presets()`, `levels("rain")`, `params()`, `setParams({ who: "gm", ground: { dryRate: 200 } })`, `setView("steep")`, `windAt(x, y)`, `status()`. Kill switch: `localStorage["mm-weather"] = "off"`.

## Dev tests

`node dev/run-all.mjs` (from the module folder, node 20+, git on the PATH) runs every node check in `dev/` (`*.test.mjs` and `*-audit.mjs`), each in its own process, and exits non-zero if any fails (`-v` prints every output). The cause test builds its "before causes" baseline from git (each cause commit against its parent, and the working tree with the cause commits taken back out), so a new commit that changes cause code goes into `CAUSE_COMMITS` in `dev/destruction-cause.test.mjs`. `dev/stream-smoke.mjs` is a live check, run in a GM's console on a test world.
